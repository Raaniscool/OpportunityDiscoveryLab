# Industry Analysis — 22 Profiles

Research cutoff: **2026-11-08**. Evidence grades: **A** official/audited data; **B** large independent survey or primary dataset with methodology; **C** vendor survey, pricing page, small sample, or secondary reporting; **D** weak/unverified. Vendor-sponsored evidence is flagged. Market sizes are directional and use different definitions; do not sum them.

Each profile contains: overview; market size with source/date; customer types; spending patterns; major problems; competitors; emerging opportunities; potential Arena products; monetization; barriers to entry; competition intensity; growth potential; evidence quality.

## 1. Accounting, tax preparation, and client advisory services (CAS)

**Overview.** Firms sell compliance, bookkeeping, payroll, and advisory work. The bottleneck is not tax logic alone; it is collecting client evidence, chasing documents, reconciling books, and turning seasonal tax work into recurring CAS.

**Market size.** U.S. accounting services: **$154.9B in 2025**, −0.2% YoY (IBISWorld, accessed 2026-11-08) [1](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-size/accounting-services/1398/). U.S. payroll & bookkeeping services: **$82.1B in 2026**, +5.4% estimated (IBISWorld, Oct 2026 report) [1](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/payroll-bookkeeping-services/1397/).

**Customer types.** Solo CPAs, 2–50-person firms, CAS/bookkeeping practices, payroll bureaus, and SMB clients who supply documents.

**Spending patterns.** Per-seat workflow software, per-return organizers, client portals, e-signature, and offshore/admin labor. Karbon Team is **$59/user/mo annual**, Business **$89** [1](https://karbonhq.com/pricing). Soraban packages start at **250 returns**, setup **$1,000–$4,500**, and claims 30+ admin hours/month saved (vendor claim) [1](https://www.soraban.com/tax-organizer-software). Liscio advertises **$19/user/mo** entry and **$49/user/mo** platform tiers (vendor comparison, Sep 2026) [1](https://www.liscio.me/resources/liscio-vs-smartvault). Content Snare is reported at **$35–$215+/mo** annual (secondary, 2026) [2](https://www.portico.run/blog/post/content-snare-pricing).

**Major problems.** Client evidence chasing, missing documents, scope creep, seasonal capacity, and cash collection. Intuit QuickBooks’ 2025 survey (n=2,487) found **56% of small businesses owed money**, average **$17,500**, and **47%** had invoices >30 days overdue [2](https://www.cnbc.com/select/how-to-paid-faster-small-business/). This is SMB-side evidence, but it explains why accountants are pulled into collections and cash-flow advisory.

**Competitors.** Karbon, Liscio, Content Snare, Soraban, SmartVault, Client Hub, QuickBooks Accountant, Xero Practice Manager, and offshore staffing firms.

**Emerging opportunities.** Year-round CAS evidence loops; AI document triage with human review; client-facing "missing items" agents; engagement profitability dashboards; notice-response assembly.

**Potential Arena products.** (O01) A client-evidence chasing and CAS close assistant that plugs into email/portal/QBO/Xero, tracks missing items per engagement, drafts reminders, and produces a monthly close packet. Not a tax-preparation engine.

**Monetization.** Per-firm base + per-active-client or per-engagement meter; premium CAS close module; implementation fee.

**Barriers to entry.** Trust, integrations, data security, seasonal switching costs, and incumbent workflow lock-in. Moderate.

**Competition intensity.** High in generic portals; medium in CAS-specific evidence reconciliation.

**Growth potential.** Medium. Accounting services are flat, but CAS/payroll is growing and firms are capacity-constrained.

**Evidence quality.** **B.** Strong market-size anchors and observed pricing; demand evidence is partly adjacent (SMB late payments) and vendor claims.

## 2. Construction and specialty subcontractors

**Overview.** General contractors and specialty trades bid, estimate, schedule, and document work. Money leaks through scope gaps, change orders, slow estimating, and document chaos.

**Market size.** U.S. construction put-in-place: **$2,164.4B SAAR in 2025**, −1.4% YoY; private **$1,647.5B**, public **$516.8B** (Census, Feb 27 2026) [1](https://www.census.gov/construction/c30/pdf/pr202512.pdf).

**Customer types.** GCs, subcontractors (electrical, mechanical, concrete, drywall), estimators, project managers, owners’ reps.

**Spending patterns.** Estimating seats, project management, document control, takeoff, and accounting. AGC/Sage 2025 survey (n≈1,109): **44% plan to increase AI spend**, **40% document management**, **36% accounting**, **35% estimating/PM** [2](https://www.agc.org/sites/default/files/users/user21902/2025%20Outlook%20Virtual%20Media%20Briefing%20Remarks.pdf). Buildxact U.S. pricing: **$199/$399/$599 per month**, AI estimate reviewer **+$149/mo** [1](https://www.buildxact.com/us/pricing/).

**Major problems.** Labor shortage (**78% hard to fill craft roles**, AGC), material cost volatility, bid scope gaps, change-order disputes, and low AI maturity. RICS 2025 global survey (n>2,200): **45% no AI use**, **34% pilots**, **<12% regular single-process use**, **<1% embedded** [1](https://www.rics.org/news-insights/artificial-intelligence-in-construction-report).

**Competitors.** Procore, Autodesk Construction Cloud, Buildxact, Togal.AI, STACK, PlanSwift, Bluebeam, Sage, Foundation, and trade-specific estimating tools.

**Emerging opportunities.** Bid-level scope reconciliation, change-order evidence capture, subcontractor prequalification, drawing/spec delta detection, and AI-assisted estimate review.

**Potential Arena products.** (O04) A subcontractor bid reconciliation tool that compares invitations, drawings, specs, and vendor quotes to flag missing scope, exclusions, and risk before bid submission.

**Monetization.** Per-seat + per-project or per-bid pack; GC-sponsored subcontractor tier.

**Barriers to entry.** Drawing parsing accuracy, liability, integration with takeoff/PM, and trust in a high-stakes bid. Medium-high.

**Competition intensity.** High in takeoff/PM; medium in scope-gap/risk reconciliation.

**Growth potential.** Medium. Total construction is flat, but public works, data centers, and water/sewer are stronger (AGC).

**Evidence quality.** **B.** Census + large industry surveys; product wedge still needs validation.

## 3. Healthcare administration and prior authorization

**Overview.** Providers and payers exchange eligibility, claims, attachments, and prior authorizations. The pain is administrative labor and care delay, not lack of clinical AI.

**Market size.** U.S. revenue cycle management market: **$172.24B in 2024**, forecast 10.1% CAGR 2025–2030 (Grand View Research, 2025) [1](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/us-revenue-cycle-management-rcm-market). CAQH/DataSpring 2025 Index: **$258B avoided in 2024**, **$21B remaining automation savings** across defined administrative transactions (Feb 19 2026) [1](https://www.dataspring.com/blog/2025-caqh-index-shows-u.s.-healthcare-avoided-258-billion-and-accelerated-automation-interoperability-and-ai-adoption).

**Customer types.** Specialty clinics, ambulatory practices, billing companies, RCM vendors, health systems, payers.

**Spending patterns.** RCM outsourcing, clearinghouse fees, prior-auth staff, EHR modules, and automation tools. AMA 2025 survey (n=1,000, Dec 2025): **40 PAs/physician/week**, **13 hours/week**, **40% of practices have dedicated PA staff** [1](https://www.ama-assn.org/press-center/ama-press-releases/ama-survey-prior-authorization-reform-pledge-falls-short-physicians).

**Major problems.** Manual evidence assembly, payer-specific requirements, denials, phone/fax workflows, and care delay. AMA: **95% report delays**, **26% report a serious adverse event** [1](https://www.ama-assn.org/press-center/ama-press-releases/ama-survey-prior-authorization-reform-pledge-falls-short-physicians). CMS-0057-F requires impacted payer prior-auth APIs primarily by **Jan 1 2027** [1](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-finalizes-rule-expand-access-health-information-improve-prior-authorization-process).

**Competitors.** Waystar, Availity, Optum, Experian Health, Cohere Health, Anterior, Rhyme, Infinitus, and EHR-native modules.

**Emerging opportunities.** Specialty-specific evidence assembly, denial appeal packets, payer API readiness, and human-reviewed submission prep.

**Potential Arena products.** (O05) A prior-auth evidence assembly tool for one specialty (e.g., orthopedics or infusion) that extracts clinical criteria, builds payer-specific packets, and tracks status. Human submits/reviews.

**Monetization.** Per-provider/month, per-authorization, or RCM-vendor white label.

**Barriers to entry.** HIPAA, EHR integrations, payer relationships, liability, long sales cycles. High.

**Competition intensity.** High and well-funded.

**Growth potential.** High. Regulatory API deadlines and persistent labor burden.

**Evidence quality.** **B.** Strong primary surveys and transaction data; commercial feasibility for a small team is the weak link.

## 4. Freight brokerage and third-party logistics

**Overview.** Brokers and 3PLs match shippers and carriers, manage exceptions, and prevent fraud. Margin is thin; fraud and unpaid accessorials are direct P&L leaks.

**Market size.** U.S. 3PL market: **$323.4B gross / $138.2B net in 2025**, +5.0%/+5.1% (Armstrong & Associates, Jun 11 2026) [2](https://www.3plogistics.com/reshaping-third-party-logistics-in-a-decade-of-structural-change-2026/).

**Customer types.** Freight brokers, 3PLs, small fleets, shippers, carrier sales reps, compliance teams.

**Spending patterns.** TMS subscriptions, carrier vetting, fraud monitoring, tracking, and factoring. MyCarrierPortal starts at **$515/mo** with unlimited users [1](https://www.mycarrierportal.com/features/pricing/). TIA Apr 2025: **22% of respondents lost >$200K to fraud in six months**, **10% spent >$200K on prevention**, **83% faced ≥3 fraud types** [1](https://news.tianet.org/tia-releases-state-of-fraud-in-the-industry-april-2025-report/).

**Major problems.** Identity spoofing, double brokering, cargo theft, detention disputes, and manual accessorial billing. ATRI 2024: detention cost **$15.1B in 2023**; **94.5% of fleets charge detention but <50% of invoices are paid** [1](https://truckingresearch.org/2024/09/new-research-documents-substantial-financial-and-safety-impacts-from-truck-driver-detention/).

**Competitors.** Highway, CarrierSource, MyCarrierPortal, Truckstop, DAT, Parade, McLeod, Turvo, DockClaim (launch pricing **$49/mo**, vendor page) [1](https://dockclaim.com/), and TMS incumbents.

**Emerging opportunities.** Carrier identity graphs, detention evidence capture, accessorial recovery, and exception-to-cash automation.

**Potential Arena products.** (O02) Detention/accessorial recovery for small fleets and brokers: ingest ELD/GPS timestamps, rate confirmations, and dock photos; generate compliant invoices and dispute packets; track payment.

**Monetization.** % of recovered accessorials + base SaaS; broker white label.

**Barriers to entry.** ELD/TMS integrations, contract terms, dispute credibility, and broker adoption. Medium.

**Competition intensity.** Medium in recovery; high in fraud/vetting.

**Growth potential.** Medium-high. Fraud spend is rising; freight volumes are cyclical.

**Evidence quality.** **B.** ATRI + TIA + Armstrong are strong; recovery conversion rate is unproven.

## 5. Transportation: small fleets and trucking operations

**Overview.** Small carriers run trucks, drivers, maintenance, compliance, and cash flow. They buy tools that protect uptime and revenue, not generic AI.

**Market size.** U.S. transportation services contributed **$1.9T (6.3% of enhanced GDP) in 2024** (BTS, Feb 24 2026) [1](https://www.bts.gov/newsroom/transportation-services-contributed-63-us-gdp-2024-down-2023-and-high-66-2022). ATA reports trucking freight bill **$906B in 2024** and **72.7% of freight by weight** [1](https://www.trucking.org/economics-and-industry-data).

**Customer types.** Owner-operators, 5–100-truck fleets, dispatchers, safety managers, maintenance shops.

**Spending patterns.** ELD, TMS, fleet maintenance, fuel cards, factoring, insurance. Fleetio is reported around **$4–$10/vehicle/mo** (secondary pricing guide, Oct 2026) [2](https://autograff.ai/blog/fleet-maintenance-software-cost). VMS small-fleet survey (Nov 11 2025, n>2,000, vendor-sponsored): **65% manage operations alone**, **83% ready to adopt new tools**, **41% estimate $10K–$50K annual downtime cost** [1](https://www.manilatimes.net/2025/11/11/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/new-survey-from-vehicle-management-systems-vms-reveals-65-of-small-fleet-managers-handle-operations-alone-83-ready-to-adopt-new-tools-92-operate-fleets-under-50-vehicles/2221225).

**Major problems.** Unpaid detention, maintenance downtime, driver turnover, insurance cost, compliance paperwork, and fraud exposure.

**Competitors.** Samsara, Motive, Fleetio, Whip Around, Truckstop, DAT, KeepTruckin legacy, and factoring portals.

**Emerging opportunities.** Maintenance-to-cash workflows, detention recovery, driver document collection, and insurance-ready safety packets.

**Potential Arena products.** (O02) Fleet-side detention recovery; (O16 adjacent) compliance packet assembly for small fleets. Avoid building a full TMS.

**Monetization.** Per-vehicle/month or success fee on recovered revenue.

**Barriers to entry.** Hardware/ELD integrations, low software budgets, high churn in small fleets. Medium.

**Competition intensity.** High in telematics; medium in revenue recovery.

**Growth potential.** Medium. Cyclical freight market; regulatory/compliance spend is sticky.

**Evidence quality.** **B** for industry size and detention; **C** for fleet software pricing/survey.

## 6. E-commerce, returns, and reverse logistics

**Overview.** Online retail is large but margin-sensitive. Returns, fraud, and exception handling are operational costs that brands and 3PLs already pay to reduce.

**Market size.** U.S. retail e-commerce: **$1,233.7B in 2025**, +5.4%, **16.4% of retail** (Census Q4 2025 release, Mar 10 2026) [1](https://www2.census.gov/retail/releases/historical/ecomm/25q4.pdf).

**Customer types.** DTC brands, marketplace sellers, 3PLs, returns processors, apparel/footwear merchants.

**Spending patterns.** Returns platforms, WMS modules, fraud tools, customer service labor. Loop Returns starts at **$340/mo** for up to 1,000 returns (official pricing, accessed 2026-11-08) [1](https://www.loopreturns.com/pricing/). Radial 2025 survey (n=200, Jul 2025): **44% cite fraud/abuse as top returns pain**, **91% of apparel refunds issued before inspection**, **48% handle returns in-house** [1](https://www.radial.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/State-of-Returns_Report_2025_Final.pdf).

**Major problems.** Return fraud, blind refunds, inspection labor, disposal vs. restock decisions, and cross-border duty changes. CBP suspended duty-free de minimis treatment effective **Aug 29 2025**, with statutory repeal from **Jul 1 2027** [3](https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/260708_bulletin_vol_60_no_18.pdf).

**Competitors.** Loop, Narvar, AfterShip, ReturnGO, Two Boxes, Happy Returns, Optoro, and WMS-native returns.

**Emerging opportunities.** Returns exception evidence, inspection-to-refund automation, fraud pattern graphs, and duty-aware reverse logistics.

**Potential Arena products.** (O07) A returns exception evidence layer for 3PLs: capture photos/serials/condition, link to order and policy, generate refund/deny/restock recommendations, and produce dispute-ready packets.

**Monetization.** Per-return or per-warehouse tier + fraud analytics.

**Barriers to entry.** WMS/integration depth, warehouse hardware, policy liability. Medium.

**Competition intensity.** High in consumer returns portals; medium in 3PL exception evidence.

**Growth potential.** Medium. E-commerce grows mid-single digits; returns complexity grows faster.

**Evidence quality.** **B.** Census + Radial survey (vendor-sponsored) + observed pricing.

## 7. Software development and AI-built applications

**Overview.** AI coding tools increase output, but trust, security, and release readiness lag. The opportunity is assurance around AI-generated software, not another code generator.

**Market size.** Worldwide software spending forecast **$1.232T in 2025**, +10.5% (Gartner, Jul 15 2025) [1](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-15-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-it-spending-to-grow-7-point-9-percent-in-2025). Enterprise genAI spend: **$37B in 2025**, apps **$19B**, coding **$4B** (Menlo Ventures, Dec 9 2025) [1](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/).

**Customer types.** Professional developers, AI-native startups, agencies, internal IT, "citizen builders" using Lovable/Bolt/Replit/Cursor.

**Spending patterns.** IDE/copilot seats, security scanners, code review, CI/CD. Snyk Team from **$25/mo** (up to 10 devs) [1](https://snyk.io/plans/); CodeRabbit **$24/$48/$72 per user/mo annual** [1](https://www.coderabbit.ai/pricing).

**Major problems.** "Almost right" code, debugging time, security flaws, and release risk. Stack Overflow 2025 (n>49,000): **66% frustrated by almost-right AI code**, **45% say debugging AI code takes longer** [1](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025). Veracode 2025: **45% of AI code samples failed security tests** across 100+ LLMs [1](https://www.veracode.com/blog/genai-code-security-report).

**Competitors.** GitHub Copilot, Cursor, CodeRabbit, Snyk, Sonar, Veracode, Semgrep, DeepSource, and AI app builders’ native checks.

**Emerging opportunities.** AI-built app release audits, security regression packs, dependency/license checks, and "production readiness" reports for non-expert builders.

**Potential Arena products.** (O03) A release-readiness audit for AI-built apps: scan repo, run security/config checks, generate a human-readable risk report and fix plan, and gate deployment.

**Monetization.** Per-repo/per-scan, monthly assurance subscription, agency white label.

**Barriers to entry.** False positives, trust, integration with builders, and liability. Medium.

**Competition intensity.** High in scanners; medium in packaged AI-app release assurance.

**Growth potential.** High. AI coding adoption is expanding faster than assurance practices.

**Evidence quality.** **B.** Large developer survey + security study + observed pricing.

## 8. Home services and local SMB operations

**Overview.** Plumbers, HVAC, electricians, cleaners, and landscapers lose revenue through missed calls, slow estimates, and poor follow-up. They already buy field-service software.

**Market size.** U.S. small businesses: **36.2M**, **99.9% of firms**, ~46% of private-sector employment (SBA Office of Advocacy, Jun 30 2025) [1](https://advocacy.sba.gov/2025/06/30/new-advocacy-report-shows-the-number-of-small-businesses-in-the-u-s-exceeds-36-million/). This is a population anchor, not a software TAM.

**Customer types.** Owner-operators, 2–50-employee trades, franchise locations, dispatchers.

**Spending patterns.** FSM subscriptions, call answering, review management, ads. Jobber Core **$49/mo**, Connect **$139**, Grow **$199** (1 user, no commitment) [1](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/). Housecall Pro Basic **$59/mo annual**; claims 200K+ pros [1](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/).

**Major problems.** Missed calls, estimate follow-up, scheduling, reviews, and cash collection. Invoca 2026 benchmarks (70M+ calls): home services **52% of calls answered by a person**, **38% of answered calls are leads**, **45% of leads convert on the call** (vendor dataset) [1](https://www.invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026). NFIB 2025 tech survey (n=521): **48% of small employers see no tech advantage** over competitors [1](https://www.nfib.com/news/press-release/new-nfib-report-how-small-businesses-incorporate-tech-and-ai-advancements/).

**Competitors.** Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Workiz, FieldPulse, Podium, Chiirp, and native AI receptionists.

**Emerging opportunities.** Estimate-to-cash follow-up, missed-call recovery, review/request automation, and AI receptionist orchestration.

**Potential Arena products.** (O08) An estimate follow-up and revenue recovery layer that sits on top of Jobber/HCP/QuickBooks, sequences reminders, and surfaces stale high-value estimates.

**Monetization.** Per-location/month + success fee on recovered jobs.

**Barriers to entry.** FSM integrations, low ACV, churn, and incumbent AI features. Medium.

**Competition intensity.** High. Jobber/HCP already ship AI receptionist features.

**Growth potential.** Medium. Large population, but crowded and price-sensitive.

**Evidence quality.** **B** for call benchmarks (vendor); **C** for SMB tech survey sample.

## 9. Real estate and property management

**Overview.** Landlords and managers handle leasing, rent, maintenance, and compliance. Maintenance coordination is a recurring, measurable cost center.

**Market size.** U.S. property management industry: **$139.9B in 2026**, +1.9% (IBISWorld, May 2026) [1](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/property-management/1356).

**Customer types.** DIY landlords, 50–5,000-unit managers, HOAs, student housing, short-term rental operators.

**Spending patterns.** Per-unit/per-door software, maintenance coordination, leasing CRM. Property Meld Ops **$2.00/unit/mo**; MAX On-Call is a per-unit add-on (official page; secondary reports ~$1.50/unit), AI triage included [1](https://propertymeld.com/pricing/). Avail Unlimited Plus reported **$9/unit/mo** (secondary, 2026) [1](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/turbotenant-vs-avail/).

**Major problems.** Maintenance triage, vendor dispatch after hours, tenant communication, turnover cost, and compliance documentation.

**Competitors.** AppFolio, Buildium, Yardi, Entrata, Property Meld, Lula, Maintenance Care, TurboTenant, Avail.

**Emerging opportunities.** AI maintenance triage, vendor performance scoring, turnover punch-list automation, and insurance-ready documentation.

**Potential Arena products.** (O09) A maintenance triage and vendor coordination layer for mid-size managers that integrates with existing PMS rather than replacing it.

**Monetization.** Per-unit/month + vendor network fee.

**Barriers to entry.** PMS integrations, vendor network density, liability for emergency triage. Medium.

**Competition intensity.** High. Property Meld is a strong, priced incumbent.

**Growth potential.** Medium. Sticky per-unit revenue but slow switching.

**Evidence quality.** **B.** Market size + official incumbent pricing; demand evidence is inferred from pricing and category existence.

## 10. Education and special education administration

**Overview.** K-12 spending is large, but procurement is slow. Special education paperwork (IEP/504) is a high-burden, compliance-sensitive wedge.

**Market size.** U.S. public elementary-secondary current spending: **$842.3B FY2024**, **$17,619/pupil**; support services **$305.2B** (Census, May 7 2026) [1](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/school-system-finances.html).

**Customer types.** Districts, special education directors, school psychologists, teachers, co-ops.

**Spending patterns.** IEP systems, assessment tools, professional development, compliance software. Frontline IEP Management listed at **$9,500 for 1–1,050 students** in an Omnia cooperative contract price list (Apr 20 2026) [2](https://www.omniapartners.com/suppliers-files/E-J/Frontline_Education/Contract_Documents/R240306/R240306_Frontline_PRC_2026_04_20.pdf). MagicSchool Plus reported at **$8.33/mo annual / $12.99 monthly**, with custom district pricing (secondary reviews, Jun 2026; official pricing page returned a cookie wall) [2](https://fast.io/resources/magic-ai-review-2026/).

**Major problems.** IEP paperwork, meeting prep, compliance deadlines, privacy, and teacher workload. CDT Oct 2025: **57% of licensed special education teachers used AI for IEP/504**, up from 39%; **15% fully delegate without review** [1](https://cdt.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FINAL-CDT-2025-Hand-in-Hand-Polling-100225-accessible.pdf). Gallup/Walton Jun 2025 (n=2,232): **60% of teachers use AI**, weekly users save **5.9 hours/week** self-reported [1](https://news.gallup.com/poll/691967/three-teachers-weekly-saving-six-weeks-year.aspx).

**Competitors.** Frontline, MagicSchool, SchoolAI, Diffit, Brisk, Playground IEP, EmbraceIEP, and district-built templates.

**Emerging opportunities.** IEP compliance checking, meeting prep packets, goal/progress alignment, and privacy-safe drafting with human review.

**Potential Arena products.** (O12) An IEP compliance and meeting-prep assistant that checks drafts against state/district rules, assembles evidence, and never auto-submits.

**Monetization.** Per-district/per-student tier; co-op purchasing.

**Barriers to entry.** FERPA/IDEA, procurement cycles, state specificity, trust. High.

**Competition intensity.** Medium-high; AI teacher tools are crowded.

**Growth potential.** Medium. Budgets are stable but slow; compliance pain is persistent.

**Evidence quality.** **B.** Census + CDT + Gallup; pricing evidence is weaker.

## 11. Manufacturing and custom job shops

**Overview.** Small manufacturers quote custom parts, manage compliance, and schedule production. Quoting speed and accuracy directly affect win rate.

**Market size.** U.S. manufacturing value added: **$2.951T annualized Q3 2025** (BEA, Jan 22 2026) [1](https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/2026-01/gdp3q25-updated.pdf).

**Customer types.** CNC/machine shops, fabricators, injection molders, contract manufacturers, procurement buyers.

**Spending patterns.** ERP/MES, quoting software, CAD/CAM, compliance consulting. NAM Q4 2025: **72.1% need skilled production workers**, **69.9% positive outlook** [1](https://nam.org/wp-content/uploads/securepdfs/2025/12/NAM_Q4_2025_Outlook_Write_Up.pdf). Paperless Parts 2025 benchmark surveyed 200 custom manufacturers [1](https://www.paperlessparts.com/resources/2025-manufacturing-industry-benchmark-report/).

**Major problems.** RFQ turnaround, estimating labor, tariff/compliance uncertainty, workforce shortage, and traceability. FDA food traceability enforcement delayed to **Jul 20 2028** [2](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/28/2026-10603/challenges-and-solutions-in-lot-level-food-traceability-public-meeting-and-request-for-comments).

**Competitors.** Paperless Parts, ProShop, Fulcrum, JobBOSS, E2, Siemens, Autodesk Fusion, and ERP vendors.

**Emerging opportunities.** RFQ triage, historical quote search, compliance packet assembly, and supplier risk summaries.

**Potential Arena products.** (O11) An RFQ/compliance triage assistant for job shops that extracts requirements from drawings/emails, flags missing certs, and drafts quote assumptions.

**Monetization.** Per-shop/month + per-RFQ volume.

**Barriers to entry.** CAD/drawing accuracy, ITAR/security, ERP integration, shop-floor trust. Medium-high.

**Competition intensity.** Medium-high; Paperless Parts is entrenched.

**Growth potential.** Medium. Reshoring/tariffs create volatility; labor shortage persists.

**Evidence quality.** **B.** BEA + NAM + vendor benchmark.

## 12. Digital marketplaces and multichannel sellers

**Overview.** Brands sell across Amazon, Walmart, Mirakl-powered retail marketplaces, and dropship programs. Catalog quality and onboarding speed drive GMV.

**Market size.** Mirakl Platform GMV: **$14.6B in 2025**, +31%; Mirakl ARR **$218M** (company results, Feb 26 2026) [1](https://www.mirakl.com/news/mirakl-momentum-2025-key-results-launch-mirakl-nexus-agentic-commerce/). This is a platform subset, not total marketplace GMV.

**Customer types.** Marketplace operators, brands, distributors, 3P sellers, catalog teams.

**Spending patterns.** Marketplace platform fees, feed management, PIM, onboarding services. Mirakl 2026 Seller Report: median onboarding **28 days**; Catalog Transformer users generate **88% higher GMV** (vendor claim) [1](https://www.mirakl.com/blog/the-marketplace-revolution-key-insights-from-our-2026-seller-report).

**Major problems.** Catalog normalization, category compliance, seller onboarding, content quality, and agentic-commerce readiness.

## Cross-industry pattern

The strongest opportunities share four traits:

1. **Money already leaks** (unpaid invoices, detention, returns fraud, missed calls, admin labor).
2. **Evidence is fragmented** (documents, photos, timestamps, emails, PDFs).
3. **A human must remain accountable** (accountant, estimator, clinician, teacher, operator).
4. **A narrow wedge can be sold before a platform** (one workflow, one buyer, one measurable outcome).

Weak opportunities share the opposite: large user counts, free incumbents, unclear payer, generic AI positioning, or hit-driven budgets.
