# Monetization research

## What people pay for, and why

Published prices demonstrate what vendors **ask**, not what a particular prospect actually pays or a segment's conversion rate. Buyers pay for a result: fewer missed deadlines, revenue protection, trusted records, access to an audience, or enjoyable entertainment. Test the avoided cost against incumbent software and doing nothing, not against a theoretical market size.

| Model / real example | Public anchor | Implication for a small entrant |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription, analytics: Plausible | Starts at $9/month [S1] | Customers may pay for simplicity and privacy even against free GA; low ARPU makes paid acquisition difficult. |
| Freemium + seat/usage: Better Stack | Free 10 monitors; incident management starts at $29/license/month annual billing [S2] | A powerful free incumbent makes a generic monitor a weak wedge; specialize in actionable exceptions. |
| AI subscription + credits: GitHub Copilot | Pro $10/month, Pro+ $39/month; usage allowances [S3] | Low-cost bundled generation compresses commodity assistant prices; usage caps protect margins. |
| Commerce platform apps: Shopify | Eligible developers pay 0% revenue share on first $1m qualifying lifetime revenue from 2025, then 15%; 2.9% processing and $19 registration apply [S4] | Native discovery and billing help, but rankings, reviews, platform rules and uninstall churn dominate. |
| Payments and marketplaces: Stripe Connect | Platform-handled pricing starts at 2.9% + $0.30 per successful card charge, with other possible fees [S5] | Take rate is not gross profit; refunds, payouts, disputes and marketplace liquidity can eliminate it. |
| AI usage-based: OpenAI GPT-4.1 mini | $0.40/million input tokens and $1.60/million output tokens on model page [S6] | 100k input + 20k output tokens costs $0.072 before retries, storage, review and support; prices/models change, and the page marks this model deprecated. |
| Premium game | Heat Signature developer discussed $15 pricing and player feedback; price response was uncertain [S7] | One-time pricing fits finite entertainment, but successful launches are not representative. |

Card processing example for direct sales: Stripe publishes 2.9% + $0.30 per successful domestic card charge [S8]. Other billing, tax, currency and dispute costs may apply. See financial scenarios for explicit assumed costs, not universal rates.

## Retention and acquisition are not free

A Shopify app owner reported roughly 80 uninstalls and 60 installs monthly on an existing business [S9]. This is a self-reported single case, not a benchmark or validated financial statement. An Everhour founder described 500 payers from 30,000+ active users on a particular free offering and painful pricing-tier changes [S10]; cohort timing and definitions are unspecified. These anecdotes show that free usage, paid conversion and retention are different things. Track qualified lead → demo → paid, fully loaded acquisition cost including founder time, activation, gross churn, expansion, contribution margin and cohort payback; avoid using ARR as cash or profit.

## Failure and trust

Bench abruptly shut down in December 2024, temporarily restricting access to accounting records; Employer.com subsequently acquired and revived it [S11, S12]. Reporting describes automation delays and debt pressures but does not establish one definitive cause [S13]. Even established demand and funding did not ensure survival. Offer export, backup, clear SLAs, and a transition plan; measure manual labor rather than assume AI replaces it. A separate founder's sprint-demo startup postmortem reports overbuilding, integration complexity and shutdown [S14]; unverified self-report, not a market-wide rate.

## AI and future economics: challenge the premise

**Hypothesis:** code generation will reduce the value of basic applications, including simple dashboards and CRUD tools. Custom internal apps may replace generic licenses where requirements are stable and internal ownership is cheap. Increased supply also increases competition for distribution. **Counterevidence:** Microsoft's FY25 Q4 call reported 20 million GitHub Copilot users, not 20 million paying customers, and enterprise customer growth [S15]. Paid Copilot and Plausible pricing [S1, S3] show vendors still sell recurring software, not proof every subscription survives. A company's own adoption metrics and a few price pages cannot isolate AI's causal effect on software demand or profitability.

The cost of generating code is not the full cost of maintaining permissions, backups, uptime, integrations, security patches, support, contracts and data accuracy. Subscriptions remain rational when an ongoing responsibility is demonstrably valuable; a one-time license is preferable for static offline tools. Customization can increase lock-in and support burden, so charge setup separately, document scope and avoid unlimited obligations. Infrastructure/maintenance demand may grow as the installed base of generated apps grows, but platform bundles can also capture it. Defensibility comes from trusted distribution, specific workflow expertise, proprietary consented data, operational execution and integrations—not a model wrapper alone. Trust and distribution are advantages to *earn*, not assumed moats.

Possible models: capped usage plus base fee, per verified exception, paid setup plus maintenance, annual updates on an owned license, or outcome fees with measurable baselines. Outcome fees invite attribution disputes; avoid charging for unverified savings. AI reduces prototype time but introduces data privacy, hallucination, vendor changes, variable inference, human review and liability costs. No forecast here assumes AI gains translate automatically into adoption.
