## Executive finding

The most promising discoveries in this scan are not generic AI apps. They are **narrow, accountable workflows that convert scattered records into accepted evidence or a completed handoff**. Truck drivers need compensation prerequisites met, disaster claimants need receipts matched to inventories, and game teams need translators to see runtime context. Improving AI can reduce document and image-processing effort, but another party still has to trust the result.

The first three deserve investigation before development:

| Rank | Discovery | Why investigate | Main unresolved question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | P13: detention-pay administration | Driver and broker perspectives reveal a specific notice/evidence workflow | Do complete, timely packets actually change payment outcomes? |
| 2 | P07: disaster contents reconciliation | Concrete repeated clerical tasks beyond initial inventory capture | Can a lawful administrative service earn professional-channel acceptance? |
| 3 | P18: runtime localization QA | Detailed account of custom engineering and missing translation context | What does existing tooling already solve, and can one engine offer a repeatable wedge? |

Supporting evidence and actual links: **P13/S30–32, P07/S18–19, P18/S41–42** in the database/register. Ranking is relative to this sample, not proof these are the best opportunities on the internet.

Ranks 4–10: scope-change approval capture; broker-assisted insurance-document correction; restaurant allergen change control; therapy documentation completeness; prior-authorization coordination; accessible PDF/form task completion; and craft-category GPSR documentation. All remain **moderate or lower commercial confidence** despite frequently strong pain evidence.

## What surprised me

### 1. Capture is often already solved; acceptance is not

Spreadsheets, phone photos, ELD tracking, screenshot uploads and OCR already exist. The harder layer is checking the record against contractual requirements, following up on missing items and obtaining acceptance from a broker, insurer, customer or reviewer. That favors exception-handling services and integrations over another dashboard. This is an interpretation across P01, P07, P10, P11, P13 and P18, not a measured universal market trend.

### 2. The strongest counterevidence came from actual users

Some property managers say their existing platform handles COIs and the task is routine (S02). Bakers recommend existing costing tools (S63). Researchers praise Covidence (S35). A complaint about a workflow does not establish an unmet product gap; a well-configured incumbent or paid setup/training may be the better answer.

### 3. High human importance does not ensure a paying market

Care coordination, food-allergy checking, accessible documents and quiet-venue discovery have consequential personal value. Adoption, safety, data freshness and the separation between beneficiary and budget owner make commercial delivery difficult. Institutional sponsorship or a service model may be more appropriate than direct consumer subscriptions. That payer thesis is not yet validated.

### 4. Frustration at a paywall can be evidence against an opportunity

Splitwise review complaints show dissatisfaction, but several people prefer free alternatives and dislike recurring fees for occasional use (S70). “People hate the incumbent” is not equivalent to “people will pay a new entrant.” P32 is retained as a real problem, not promoted into a strong business.

### 5. Some losses cannot be fixed by better documentation

Therapy recoupments may involve eligibility rather than note quality (S23). Chargeback outcomes depend on reasons/liability, not just more photos (S24–25). Waiting at a dock does not necessarily create a contractual right to payment (S31). Product claims must exclude causes outside the proposed workflow.

## Strategic consideration: improving AI

This research considers both the supply of solutions and changes in the problems themselves.

| AI effect | Examples from database | Commercial implication (hypothesis) |
|---|---|---|
| Makes previously laborious evidence work cheaper | Claim receipts, label changes, historical scripts, freezer labels, localization screenshots | Deliver an outcome with human verification; raw extraction will face price pressure |
| Creates or amplifies verification needs | Freelance application spam, transformed copied patterns, voice impersonation, disputed authorship | Independent-channel verification, actual task demonstrations and rights/provenance beat superficial AI detectors |
| Expands authority-related risks | Tool-using agents reading hostile content | Permission boundaries and approved data flows matter even as model reasoning improves |
| Absorbs generic software features | Summaries, receipt entry, basic trace triage, raw translation | A thin model wrapper has little defensibility; test whether incumbents already include it |
| Can eliminate a problem | Better printer auto-calibration, improved OCR, better incumbent integrations | Recheck need frequently; do not build around a temporary capability gap |
| Adds opposing automation | Insurer-side authorization processes, automated marketplace/dispute handling | More fluent generated submissions need not improve outcomes; acceptance and institutional incentives remain limiting |

Potential value may accrue to **managed services, trusted intermediaries, expert review networks, implementation work or physical-world verification**, not necessarily recurring software subscriptions. Conversely, human review and field operations can destroy margins. No costed delivery model has been demonstrated here.

**Durability test:** If general AI became much cheaper and better tomorrow, would buyers still pay for this workflow's permissions, authenticated evidence, integration, expert accountability or actual completion? If not, the proposed product may disappear as a standalone business.

## Method and evidence limits

1. **Broad purposive discovery:** public searches across consumer, caregiver, student/teacher, creator, gamer/developer, trades, small business, transport, research and IT communities. Did not use previous user projects/interests to constrain topics.
2. **Evidence-first inclusion:** retained expressed pains, concrete workarounds, requests, incident accounts or documented technical failures. Closely related caregiver and research workflows were consolidated rather than counted twice. Weak standalone leads (e.g. general cottage-food guidance, simple HVAC manual search) were not promoted to fill the count.
3. **Triangulation:** where useful, compare customers and operators, positive and negative incumbent experiences, and official/sector sources. Seventy URLs do not mean seventy independent customers.
4. **Separate observation from inference:** evidence fields report what people/sources say; gaps, candidate responses, proposed prices and AI effects are researcher hypotheses. Frequencies are qualitative unless attributed. No fabricated market statistics, TAM, revenue forecasts or conversion rates.
5. **Evidence access:** all Reddit accounts were inspected through retrieved search excerpts, sometimes detailed, **not full authenticated thread reads**. Five attempted direct Reddit fetches returned HTTP 403 (marked S02, S04, S18, S30, S41). Full text was inspected for Zotero, one dependency issue, SoundPrint store pages, Trustpilot, FSA guidance and the opening section of Jisc's long 2026 analysis. Inspected chunks are recorded. Full text of linked papers/media was not independently verified.
6. **Source quality:** firsthand claims may be mistaken, promotional, duplicated or fabricated. Vendor replies, research solicitations and automated issues are not treated as independent buying demand. Screenshots, monetary losses and alleged wrongdoing were not independently authenticated. Historical closed issues do not establish current bugs.
7. **Review bias and freshness:** app/product reviews are self-selected; page text may duplicate reviews. Current SoundPrint release notes describe improvements, and current Jisc analysis describes improving detectors; older complaints should not be mistaken for proof nothing changed. Dynamic limits/prices/ratings can vary. No population conclusions drawn from review totals.
8. **Competition:** alternatives are identified, but current feature/pricing audits are incomplete. A candidate gap may be configuration, training or process rather than a new product. Direct demonstrations/quotes are required before commercial conclusions.
9. **Scope gaps:** social discussion predominantly Reddit; public GitHub issues, Zotero forum, App Store, Play Store, Trustpilot, official guidance and sector analysis add breadth. No private forums, authenticated social feeds, paid databases, original interviews or transactional demand data. Geographic/language coverage is biased toward English-speaking US/UK/EU discussions.
10. **Safety and lawful scope:** this is not legal, insurance, medical, educational or food-safety advice. Authoritative rules, professional responsibilities, lawful data access, consent and privacy controls require specialist verification. AI must not fabricate evidence, clinical necessity, safety clearance, rights ownership or approval.

### Confidence and difficulty

- **Pain H:** specific firsthand accounts with recurrence/corroboration or strong direct technical artifacts; not proof of prevalence.
- **Pain M:** smaller, older or more indirect sample; concern plausible but less established.
- **Commercial M:** identifiable payer and value hypothesis, with major untested assumptions.
- **Commercial L:** payer, unmet gap, differentiation or willingness-to-pay particularly weak.
- No opportunity receives high commercial confidence because no interviews, paid commitments, unit economics or full competitor checks were completed.
- Technical/delivery difficulty ranges from low–medium ledger/calibration work to very high regulated integrations, assistive-document semantics and adversarial security. These are relative judgments, not engineering schedules.

## Recommended next stage — still research, not development

Start artifact-based interviews for ranks 1–3, including satisfied incumbent users and counterparties who can reject the output. Ask about the **last actual incident**, a bounded recent frequency, current spend, workaround, decision maker, lawful access and reason not to buy. With permission, manually analyze redacted historical records. Test the stop criteria in the ranked report before seeking any product-development authorization.

Do not treat a positive interview as a sale. Seek a clear budgeted commitment only after defining the safe service boundary, accepted output and cost of human review. If a cheap incumbent configuration or process change solves the pain, record that as the research result and move on.
