All pricing and validation thresholds below are hypotheses. Supporting direct links and source limitations are in the [database](problem_database.md) and [source register](research_sources.md).

## Ranking method

Five ordinal criteria, each 1–5, equally weighted; **total out of 25**. Higher is better. This is a researcher judgment for prioritizing interviews, not a statistical model or expected-return calculation.

- **U — urgency/recurrence:** repeated operational burden or consequential failure.
- **B — buyer/economics:** identifiable payer and credible route to measurable value; not verified willingness to pay.
- **W — narrow wedge:** plausible underserved workflow despite existing alternatives.
- **F — delivery feasibility:** higher means easier to deliver responsibly, including permissions, human labor, safety and regulation.
- **E — evidence:** specificity, corroboration and counterevidence considered. No shortlist entry gets 5: most firsthand accounts are search extracts, not interviews/full-thread verification.

Ties are ordered by narrower measurable outcome, then accessible validation. Small score differences should not guide investment. Difficulty, evidence access and incumbent feature checks could readily reorder the list.

## 1. Detention administration, not another GPS tracker — P13

**Discovery:** A driver describes a long wait with compensation denied after a missing time-out signature; broker discussion makes clear that timely notice and contract conditions matter, not merely location. Official research corroborates detention as an operational measurement issue. See **S30–S32**.

**Customer/outcome:** Small carrier already experiencing repeated rejected claims; payer is carrier owner/dispatcher. Desired outcome: fewer administratively invalid submissions and less staff effort, not a promise every wait is payable.

**Why this wedge:** Start with extracting the rate-confirmation conditions, prompting notices and assembling authentic evidence. ELD/TMS platforms already supply tracking; do not compete on another map. Broker acceptance is more important than narrative quality.

**Business hypothesis:** $15–40/truck/month or fixed administrative case fee, subject to review of collections/legal boundaries. AI can lower term-reading and document-sorting cost. Human approval and reliable records remain necessary.

**Next research:** Interview 6 carriers and 4 brokers; review 30 redacted claims, including complete-evidence denials. Record denial reason, missed prerequisites, admin time and existing TMS capabilities. Seek a written, nonbinding statement of willingness to trial a paid administrative service only after the service scope is clear.

**Go/stop criteria (proposed):** Proceed only if at least 3 carriers show recurring preventable omissions and 2 brokers explicitly accept the proposed evidence format. Stop if most denials are nonpayment policy or contract ineligibility rather than fixable administration. Do not automate threats or bond claims.

## 2. Claimant receipt reconciliation after disaster — P07

**Discovery:** Claimants describe maintaining item IDs, actual cash value, replacement costs, receipt links and recoverable depreciation over time. This is more specific than “take a home inventory video.” See **S18–S19**.

**Customer/outcome:** Restoration/authorized claims professional with repeated cases; household is beneficiary and possible episodic payer. Outcome is an accurate, traceable replacement schedule and fewer missing/unmatched documents, not a larger guaranteed settlement.

**Why this wedge:** Capture has many substitutes. The candidate gap is reconciling purchases after loss against a changing insurer schedule. A professional channel may overcome episodic customer acquisition, but is unvalidated.

**Business hypothesis:** Fixed $100–500 administrative package or per-case professional license. AI extracts receipt lines and proposes item matches; claimants confirm ownership, age and identity. Preserve original evidence and never generate unsupported losses or prices.

**Next research:** Interview 5 recent claimants and 3 licensed claims professionals. Manually reconstruct one permissioned historical schedule; have a professional review every match. Obtain insurer acceptance requirements and legal advice on administrative work versus regulated adjusting.

**Go/stop criteria:** Proceed only with a lawful scope, professional partner and at least 2 buyers identifying a repeatable reconciliation budget. Stop if it requires unauthorized claim negotiation, insurer exports cannot be accepted, or manual review erases savings. Do not seek disaster victims through manipulative urgency.

## 3. Runtime localization context and regression QA — P18

**Discovery:** A developer describes custom context/string tooling; another account describes gameplay terminology errors and overflow. Existing localization platforms already offer screenshots and glossaries. See **S41–S42**.

**Customer/outcome:** Studio with multiple locales and repeated releases, or an LQA agency. Outcome is fewer context/overflow exceptions reaching players and reduced translator clarification effort.

**Why this wedge:** Focus one engine and runtime context capture plus actual-build checks. A generic translation wrapper or screenshot uploader duplicates incumbents. Current integration coverage must be directly inspected before assuming a gap.

**Business hypothesis:** Per-release reviewed QA package, later $100–500/studio/month if recurrent. Better multimodal AI helps triage visual and semantic exceptions; native-language reviewers retain final decisions.

**Next research:** Interview 5 studios and 3 agencies. Inspect a prior release’s rework with permission. Compare context collection and changed-string handling against Crowdin/Lokalise and engine integrations. Test analysis manually on existing permitted artifacts, not a newly developed product.

**Go/stop criteria:** Proceed if 3 studios show the same unsolved runtime-context task and one agency sees a paid distribution fit. Stop if existing integrations already cover it cheaply or each game needs bespoke engineering with no repeatable service margin.

## 4. Capture scope changes before crews act — P11

**Discovery:** Both customer and contractor accounts describe disagreement over whether changed work and price were authorized. A rejected emailed quote later billed illustrates that mere message storage is insufficient. See **S26–S27**.

**Customer/outcome:** Remodeling contractor with disputed extras; outcome is explicit authorized scope/price/time decisions before work and a clear exception trail.

**Why this wedge:** PM suites and e-sign tools already generate change orders. The unverified opportunity is reducing field-to-approval friction in a narrow trade. AI may identify possible scope changes but must never infer approval from silence.

**Business hypothesis:** $30–100/company/month or implementation/training service bundled with existing tools. Adoption may create more value than new software.

**Next research:** Review last disputed change at 8 remodelers, current tools and who can authorize work. Ask homeowners whether the proposed steps improve clarity rather than pressure them.

**Go/stop criteria:** Proceed if at least 3 contractors already attempt documented approvals but abandon them due to solvable field friction. Stop if disputes mostly arise from intentional noncompliance, customer refusal or the contractor declining to wait for approval. Check current tools first.

## 5. COI correction/chasing through brokers — P01

**Discovery:** Contractors describe chasing certificates and wrong/incomplete documents. Counterevidence is unusually direct: some property managers say existing software makes this routine and a new COI tool unnecessary. See **S01–S03**.

**Customer/outcome:** Small GC without adequate compliance operations; broker is potential channel/expert. Outcome is fewer unresolved paperwork exceptions and less chasing, not certified insurance coverage.

**Why this wedge:** Expiry reminders are commodity and Sheets scripts already work. Only investigate the correction loop, contract requirements and expert escalation. A certificate is not an insurance policy or proof of all endorsements/cancellation status.

**Business hypothesis:** $100–400/month managed service or broker bundle. AI improves data extraction; authoritative review remains a cost center and possible differentiator.

**Next research:** 8 GCs, 3 brokers; review correction threads. Directly compare JobTread/houndDog, Procore and broker services, including onboarding cost and capabilities—not just list prices.

**Go/stop criteria:** Proceed if 3 otherwise poorly served firms can show repeated correction effort and a broker accepts review responsibility within a lawful scope. Stop if broker inclusion or a cheap integration already addresses it. Never auto-hold payment without explicit authorized rules and legal review.

## 6. Restaurant allergen change control — P03

**Discovery:** Operators keep ingredient folders/guides; official guidance stresses accurate, current information and supplier checks. Direct willingness to pay is weaker than the safety rationale. See **S07–S09**.

**Customer/outcome:** Independent operator with substitutions or menu changes; food-safety consultant as channel. Outcome is reviewed ingredient changes propagated to affected dishes and staff instructions before service.

**Why this wedge:** A live change-control process, not a static matrix or “allergen chatbot.” Recipe platforms may already provide propagation; test first. Cross-contact requires on-site knowledge, not ingredient inference.

**Business hypothesis:** $50–150/location/month reviewed monitoring, likely consultant-led. AI label comparison makes the manual workload cheaper; chef approval remains mandatory.

**Next research:** Observe deliveries in 5 kitchens; inspect substitution records and existing tools. Have a qualified food-safety expert label past ingredient changes and process failures.

**Go/stop criteria:** Proceed only if 3 kitchens have repeated changes not captured by existing controls and managers will fund reviewed exception handling. Stop if staff do not capture deliveries, review capacity cannot scale, or safety depends on unverified AI-negative findings. No “safe to eat” guarantees.

## 7. Therapy documentation completeness, not another note generator — P09

**Discovery:** Clinicians receive conflicting audit advice. Counterevidence matters: some recoupments arise from eligibility or undisclosed other coverage and cannot be prevented by better notes. See **S22–S23**.

**Customer/outcome:** Small insurance-taking practice or biller. Outcome is identification of missing required elements before billing; not “audit-proof” care or automatic justification of a higher code.

**Why this wedge:** Source-linked payer-rule validation and contradiction detection could complement EHR/scribes. Verify whether existing products already do it. Payer interpretation may remain contested even with complete notes.

**Business hypothesis:** $40–100/clinician/month or biller-managed chart-review service. Keep factual notes distinct from private psychotherapy notes and protect PHI.

**Next research:** Qualified biller reviews 30 consented, redacted charts against 3 current payer manuals. Separate completeness issues from clinical judgments and eligibility/coordination-of-benefits risk.

**Go/stop criteria:** Proceed with a lawful data-handling plan if 2 practices identify repeated material omissions current tools miss. Stop if rules cannot be reliably sourced or most risk is unrelated to completeness. Never alter history, invent necessity or guarantee reimbursement.

## 8. Closed-loop prior-authorization coordination — P05

**Discovery:** Patients cannot tell which party holds a request; clinicians describe repeated staff effort. Existing electronic authorization does not necessarily provide end-to-end operational closure. See **S12–S14**.

**Customer/outcome:** One specialty clinic; clinic/sponsor pays. Outcome is fewer lost handoffs and earlier renewal initiation, not clinical treatment selection or guaranteed approval.

**Why only eighth:** Pain is very strong, but integrations, PHI, staff cooperation and mature competitors make entry difficult. A human service may validate the workflow before a software thesis, and even then may not change payer decisions.

**Business hypothesis:** Retainer or completed-case admin fee. AI organizes evidence and status; clinicians approve all medical content and submissions.

**Next research:** Shadow 20 consented cases, identify time spent versus elapsed waiting, parties contacted and addressable stalls. Compare existing vendors and distinguish pharmacy versus medical benefits.

**Go/stop criteria:** Proceed only with permitted data access and 2 clinics showing controllable, recurrent handoff failures. Stop if elapsed time is mostly payer policy or unavailable data, or clinic staff will not cooperate. No unsupervised medical appeals.

## 9. Accessible document task completion — P19

**Discovery:** Blind users distinguish readable text from usable editing and forms, and rely on several conversions/workarounds. See **S43–S44**.

**Customer/outcome:** Document-issuing employer/university, not only the excluded user. Outcome is completion of the actual task with assistive technology while retaining faithful content.

**Why this wedge:** Test structured tables, reading order and form operation rather than celebrating successful OCR. Existing remediation firms and accessibility tools are serious alternatives. Use paid disabled-user expertise from the start.

**Business hypothesis:** Institution-funded per-document service or retainer, with human validation. AI helps recover structure but introduces content-fidelity risks.

**Next research:** Pay blind testers to complete permitted real forms using current tools and a manually remediated alternative. Record task barriers, corrections and review time. Interview procurement/accessibility staff.

**Go/stop criteria:** Proceed if 2 institutions identify an unmet queue and budget, and testers show meaningful task-level improvement over current services. Stop if document semantics cannot be validated reliably or the case depends on charging people for access to essential rights/services.

## 10. GPSR craft documentation service — P02

**Discovery:** Multiple sellers avoid EU sales over compliance confusion/cost; others comply after setup. Official FAQ confirms real obligations, not merely an annoying form. See **S04–S06**.

**Customer/outcome:** Physical-goods shop with enough EU gross profit to justify compliance. Outcome is a maintained, professionally reviewed documentation workflow, not guaranteed legal compliance through generated text.

**Why only tenth:** Low-value sellers may not economically justify export even with cheaper assistance. Legal accountability and representative costs cannot be eliminated by AI. Marketplace/agency bundling is a major substitution threat.

**Business hypothesis:** Setup plus $15–50/month maintenance with responsible-person services separately contracted and priced. Start one product/material category; actual fees and professional responsibilities need quotes.

**Next research:** 10 sellers’ export economics, 3 agency quotes and independent safety-professional review. Do not rely on Reddit claims about exemptions, enforcement or where sellers may lawfully trade.

**Go/stop criteria:** Proceed if 3 sellers have sufficient recurring EU margin and an authorized partner can responsibly cover the category. Stop if representative/expert cost consumes savings or marketplace services already solve the complete task.

## Research allocation and commercial discipline

Start interviews for ranks **1–3 in parallel**. Use ranks 4–6 as alternatives if current tools or inaccessible data eliminate the leading wedge. Pursue ranks 7–10 only with appropriate professional partners.

For every candidate, collect (with permission): the **last real incident**, original workflow artifacts, current alternative and actual spend, frequency over a bounded recent period, responsible budget owner, measurable desired outcome, and reasons a paid service would fail. Include satisfied incumbent users and ask what would make them switch. Do not substitute “interesting idea” survey answers for paid commitment.

Before product authorization, require lawful data/tool access, direct current competitor checks, explicit buyer commitment, costed human-review/operations assumptions, and a safe fallback for uncertain outputs. Decline a category if its economics require unsupported automation or false assurance. None of those gates has been met by this desk-research pass.
