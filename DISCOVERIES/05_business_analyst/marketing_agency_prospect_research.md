# Small-business prospect discovery: marketing agencies

Research snapshot: 8 November 2026. **Research only: no outreach, software build, or access to customer systems.**

## Executive summary

Fifteen identifiable agencies below have publicly described a reporting or data-management problem. The strongest evidence is surprisingly unfavorable to an immediate sales pitch: **all 15 sources describe solutions already adopted**, generally from the vendor publishing the case study. They establish historical pain and recurring workloads, not a currently unsolved problem, a budget, or willingness to hire us. Vendor-authored success stories are selected marketing material; their claims have not been independently audited. Current processes, employee counts, business health and decision-makers need confirmation. Historical saved hours are not guaranteed current savings, cash savings, or new revenue.

The best discovery candidate is **Market Solutions**: the case study explicitly says some reports still require additional images and screenshots after adopting Whatagraph, and identifies a 15-person team. A narrow, human-reviewed attachment workflow could be explored, but the source does not quantify current residual effort or indicate any desire for a custom tool. If the agency already has a satisfactory workaround, do not pitch. The following shortlist is a ranking of **discovery priority**, not validated purchase intent: 1 Market Solutions, 2 MyMediaMatters, 3 ImageMark, 4 Media Maven, 5 Z21 Studio. All five have low confidence as *current buyers*.

**Selection:** focused search for named agencies in vendor case studies mentioning specific workflows, paired where possible with agency sites and third-party size listings. Small means roughly <50 employees, or an owner explicitly calling the agency small; this is a research screen, not a jurisdiction-specific legal classification. Employee counts and people in older sources may have changed. No arbitrary spend, revenue or ability-to-pay claims: ability to pay is unknown, although adoption of tools or managing multiple accounts indicates operational scale, not available budget. Names below are potential research contacts/roles, not verified purchasers.

**Complexity estimates** are analyst hypotheses for one developer with permitted access and a fixed, small scope, inclusive of testing and handover, excluding sales, procurement, ongoing maintenance and inaccessible APIs. Low = 40–80 hours; medium = 80–160; high = 160–300+. Actual work can be larger or unnecessary. AI may assist coding or draft summaries, but integrations, privacy, validation and human approval remain necessary. Each item below specifies the existing alternative and a reason the owner may decline. Confidence describes confidence that a custom project is currently an actionable paid opportunity, not confidence that the historical source exists.

## Owner discovery questions (for later, with permission)

1. What was the last report or onboarding task that delayed the team? Can you walk through a redacted example and show where time is actually spent?
2. How often did that specific task occur over the last three months, and who performs and reviews it? How many minutes are truly avoidable after existing automation?
3. What did your current platform or its support team try? Which feature, integration or template is insufficient, and why? Is the problem already fixed?
4. What errors or follow-ups actually occurred, and how were they detected? What is the downside of a missed or false alert?
5. Which data and client permissions would be needed? Can a test use synthetic or sanitized data? Who owns access and approves exports, retention and security?
6. Who would evaluate and approve a small project, what budget and procurement constraints exist, and is there a willingness to pay for a bounded pilot rather than merely interest?
7. Would an off-the-shelf setting, spreadsheet or internal staff member be cheaper? What would justify choosing a custom add-on instead?
8. What baseline, acceptance criteria and one-month trial result would make this worth continuing? Who maintains it when APIs or templates change?

## Next-step decision rule

Start with Market Solutions as a **research hypothesis**, not a qualified lead. Verify that the business and personnel are current, then seek permission for discovery only when outreach is authorized. Do not access client accounts. If the owner cannot identify recurring residual effort, cannot share a sanitized example, or already solves it using Whatagraph, close the prospect and try #2. If meaningful pain exists, compare existing-tool configuration first; propose only a narrow paid, reversible pilot after the buyer agrees to measurable scope, privacy and maintenance terms.
